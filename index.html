Learned Intuition

Simulation-Based Inference, Bayesian Optimization, and the Rise of Agentic Research

Three fields, one question asked three ways. Part I is about learning to recognize an answer instead of computing it from scratch every time (amortized inference). Part II is about deciding what to try next when each real trial is expensive (Bayesian optimization). Part III asks who decides — what happens when an LLM agent, not a fixed policy, is the one steering either of the first two. The order below is a reading path, not just a filing system: each module states what it assumes you already know.

Every card below has a stable anchor and is ready for a guided walkthrough — read one, then just ask and we'll go through it together line by line.

Orientation

Shared vocabulary

Before the deep end: the same idea — train on simulated examples so a network learns to recognize the answer — was invented three times, in three communities, under three names. Read these two first so every later paper's jargon resolves to one mental model.

00

Shared vocabulary

The physics/astro lineage calls it simulation-based inference; the stats/cog-sci lineage calls it amortized Bayesian inference; the AutoML lineage calls it a prior-data fitted network. Same math, three papers below that each speak a different dialect of it — and one that translates between all three.

The Frontier of Simulation-Based Inference

Cranmer, Brehmer, Louppe·2020·PNASread first

The field-defining survey from the physics/astronomy lineage. Frames the likelihood-free inference problem, traces ABC → neural density estimation, and previews the "learn the posterior / likelihood / likelihood-ratio directly" taxonomy every later paper specializes. This is the shared vocabulary for the entire library.

Neural Methods for Amortized Inference

Zammit-Mangion, Sainsbury-Dale, Huser·2025·Annual Review of Statistics

The closest thing to a field-wide unifying survey — explicitly bridges the SBI / ABI / PFN naming split under one "amortized inference" umbrella, with a worked illustrative example. Doesn't pick a lineage, which is exactly why it's useful as a translation layer.

Part I

Recognize — Simulation-Based & Amortized Bayesian Inference

Instead of grinding out a posterior with MCMC for every new dataset, train a network on millions of simulated (parameters → data) pairs until it learns to see the answer. Five modules: how the trick works, what to build with, how to know if you can trust it, how it extends to causal questions, and where — in marketing econometrics specifically — nobody has applied it yet.

01

The mechanism

One idea, one paper of origin, then the entire tabular-foundation-model line that proves it scales. Read Müller et al. before TabPFN — it's the mechanism; TabPFN is the scaled-up, benchmark-beating application of it.

Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference

Müller, Hollmann, Pineda, Grabocka, Hutter·2022·ICLR2112.10510origin

The origin paper for Prior-Data Fitted Networks: train a transformer via in-context learning to approximate Bayesian inference over a prior, in one forward pass. TabPFN, Do-PFN, and CausalPFN all inherit this recipe directly. Shows PFNs near-perfectly mimicking Gaussian-process posteriors with 200×+ speedups.

TabPFN: Accurate Predictions on Small Data

Hollmann, Müller, Purucker, et al.·2025·Nature

The paradigm-proof: a transformer trained on ~130M synthetic tasks does in-context learning over tabular data — one forward pass beats an ensemble of GBTs tuned for 4 hours. The empirical backbone of this whole library's thesis.

TabPFN-2.5

Prior Labs·2025

Scale-up: 50,000 rows / 2,000 features — a 20× increase in data cells over TabPFN v2.

TabPFN-3: Technical Report

Prior Labs·2026

Scales to 1M training rows, adds a "Thinking" test-time-compute mode, extends to time-series, relational, and tabular-text data.

From Tables to Time: TabPFN-v2 for Time Series

Hoo, Müller·2026

Ports the tabular in-context-learning recipe to covariate-informed forecasting — external regressors like weather or economic indicators folded directly into the context.

TabICLv2

Qu, Holzmüller, Varoquaux, Le Morvan·2026current SOTA

Current open SOTA on TabArena, beating tuned CatBoost/XGBoost and RealTabPFN-2.5 with no dataset-specific tuning. Query-Aware Scalable Softmax fixes attention fading at scale; disk-offloading gets million-row tables onto modest GPUs; 999-quantile regression gives full predictive distributions, not point estimates.

Effortless, Simulation-Efficient Bayesian Inference via TFMs

Vetter, Gloeckler, Gedon, Macke·2025·NeurIPSshortcut

Uses TabPFN itself as a generic SBI engine — feed simulated (θ, x) pairs in as a "table," let in-context learning do the posterior approximation. No bespoke BayesFlow-style network to train. The fastest path to a working amortized posterior in the whole library.

Companion repos
repos/PFNs — current maintained PFN training repo, use to train on a custom prior · repos/TransformersCanDoBayesianInference — predecessor repo, contains notebooks/BayesianModels_And_Custom_Pyro_Modules.ipynb, the notebook to fork for a custom Pyro prior.
02

Core tooling

The libraries and worked examples you'd actually build with. metabeta is the dress rehearsal for an amortized hierarchical regression model — read it closely, it's structurally the closest thing here to a Marketing Mix Model.

metabeta: Bayesian Mixed-Effects Regression

Kipnis, Binz, Schulz·2025dress rehearsal

The closest existing analog to an amortized Marketing Mix Model — hierarchical (fixed + random effects) regression via neural posterior estimation, matching HMC accuracy at ~0.01s vs ~37s. Local + global summary networks, normalizing-flow posteriors, plus post-hoc importance sampling and conformal prediction for calibration.

BayesFlow

Radev, Schmitt, Schumacher, et al.·2023

Original BayesFlow paper. Establishes the prior → simulator → summary network → inference network pipeline and the "amortized Bayesian workflow" framing everything after it uses.

BayesFlow 2

Kühmichel, et al.·2026workhorse

Major rewrite on Keras 3 (JAX/PyTorch/TensorFlow). Adds diffusion/flow-matching/consistency-model inference networks, native hierarchical model support, and a first-class Diagnostics module. The primary coding-exercise tool for the ABI half of a workshop.

sbi: A Toolkit for Simulation-Based Inference

Tejero-Cantero, Boelts, Deistler, et al.·2020·JOSS

The other major SBI library (Macke lab, PyTorch). Lower-level than BayesFlow — exposes the density-estimator choice directly (NPE / NLE / NRE families) instead of one high-level API.

sbi reloaded

Boelts, Deistler, Gloeckler, Tejero-Cantero, et al.·2025

Major rewrite of sbi, expanding from an algorithm library into a full workflow toolkit — mirrors BayesFlow 2's own evolution. The two production-grade ABI frameworks, side by side.

Amortized Bayesian Mixture Models

Kucharský, Bürkner·2025

A worked ABI-workflow example on finite mixture models (label-switching, high-dim posteriors) — a gentler second exercise before metabeta-level hierarchy.

Simformer: All-in-One SBI

Gloeckler, Deistler, Weilbach, Wood, Macke·2024

Transformer-based SBI that handles missing data and flexible conditioning via masking — condition on any subset of parameters or data at inference time. Real commercial panels are rarely complete; this is that exercise.

Diffusion Models in SBI: A Tutorial Review

Arruda, Bracher, Köthe, Hasenauer·2025

The generative-modeling layer underneath BayesFlow/metabeta: diffusion, flow matching, and consistency models as alternatives to normalizing flows, plus score-based "superpowers" like prior adaptation without retraining. The best single piece on how the network represents the posterior.

Companion repo
repos/sbi — the Macke-lab toolkit, cloned and ready to run.
03

Calibration & trust

Do not skip this module. A fast posterior that's quietly wrong is worse than a slow one that's honest — for a decision feeding real budget, overconfidence is the failure mode that looks right and isn't.

Simulation-Based Calibration

Talts, Betancourt, Simpson, Vehtari, Gelman·2018diagnostic standard

The diagnostic standard. Simulate parameters → simulate data → run inference → check that rank statistics of the true parameter within the recovered posterior are uniform. Any amortized model bound for a real decision needs this before it's trusted. Hands-on exercise: implement the rank histogram from scratch.

A Trust Crisis in Simulation-Based Inference?

Hermans, Delaunoy, et al.·2022·TMLRload-bearing

Nearly every SBI algorithm tested (NPE, SNL, …) produced overconfident posteriors — credible intervals too narrow — in at least one benchmark, worst exactly in the low-simulation-budget regime where amortized methods are most tempting to reach for. Remedy: deep ensembling, 5+ independently trained networks, to separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty.

Calibrating Neural SBI with Differentiable Coverage

Falkiewicz, Takeishi, Shekhzadeh, et al.·2023

Makes coverage itself a differentiable training objective instead of a post-hoc diagnostic — bakes calibration into training rather than checking for it afterward.

Robust ABI with Self-Consistency Losses

Mishra, Habermann, Schmitt, Radev, Bürkner·2026·ICLR

Addresses the "simulation gap": what happens when real data doesn't look like the training simulations. Semi-supervised — let the model also learn from real, unlabeled data via self-consistency, improving robustness far from the simulated training distribution.

04

Causal SBI

Promising, not proven — the causal extension of the PFN recipe has synthetic and semi-synthetic evidence, real-benchmark evidence in one case, and no real-world marketing validation yet.

Do-PFN

Robertson, Reuter, Guo, Hollmann, Hutter, Schölkopf·2025·NeurIPS

Trains a transformer on millions of synthetic Structural Causal Models so it can predict conditional interventional distributions p(y | do(t), x) zero-shot, without the causal graph at inference time. Correctly performs back-door/front-door-style adjustment implicitly. Synthetic/semi-synthetic evidence only so far.

CausalPFN

Balazadeh, Kamkari, Thomas, et al.·2025

Same PFN-for-causality idea as Do-PFN, but targets the expectation (CEPO) and enforces identifiability in the training prior. Tested on real causal benchmarks (IHDP, LaLonde, ACIC) plus real uplift tasks — more real-data validation than Do-PFN.

Interventional Time Series Priors

Thumm, Chen·2026·ICLR Workshop

Extends the causal-PFN idea to time series — synthetic priors with regime-switching and time-varying interventions. Structurally the closest thing in the library to "a brand's sales trajectory under a marketing intervention."

05

Marketing & econometrics applications — the gap

Where the recipe hasn't landed yet. The incumbent, the one neural foil, and the nearest amortized-inference relative — none of them are the thing this library is arguing for.

Bayesian MMM with Carryover & Shape Effects

Jin, Wang, Sun, Chan, Koehler — Google·2017incumbent

The incumbent standard for MMM: hierarchical Bayesian regression with adstock (carryover) and Hill-function saturation, fit via MCMC, one brand at a time. Read as the baseline to benchmark against, not a method to imitate mechanically — this is the thing amortized inference would replace.

NNN: Neural Networks for Marketing Measurement

Mulc, Anderson, Cubre, Zhang, Liu, Kumar — Google·2025the foil

The one existing neural alternative to classic MMM — and it goes the opposite direction from amortized inference: a Transformer trained on a brand's own data that abandons explicit priors and parametric adstock/saturation, trading uncertainty quantification for flexibility. The counterpoint for explaining why amortized SBI (priors kept, just moved into a simulator) is a different bet.

Amortized Inference for Discrete Choice Models

Huch, Keane·2026

The only amortized-inference paper found touching marketing/econometrics directly — discrete choice, not MMM. Confirms the porting-the-PFN-recipe trend is real but hasn't reached MMM. The closest thing to prior art for this library's actual thesis.

Confirmed gap

Amortized inference for Media Mix Modeling — nothing published

There is no SBI/ABI paper for MMM specifically. The tooling exists (BayesFlow, sbi), the closest structural analog exists (metabeta), and the domain exists — nobody has connected them. This is open territory.

Part II

Decide — Bayesian Optimization

You get a handful of real, expensive trials. Which one do you run next? Three modules: the pre-LLM math everything else stands on, its causal-graph-aware extension, and what changes once an LLM is allowed to sit inside the loop.

06

BO foundations

Solid, classic, in rough order of how directly each builds on the last. No hands-on repo here yet — pull BoTorch/Ax docs when building exercises.

Efficient Global Optimization (EGO)

Jones, Schonlau, Welch·1998founding paper

Introduces Expected Improvement and Kriging (GP) surrogates for optimizing expensive black-box functions. Everything downstream in BO traces back here.

Practical Bayesian Optimization ("Spearmint")

Snoek, Larochelle, Adams·2012

Made BO practical for ML hyperparameter tuning: noisy observations, parallel/async evaluation, GP hyperparameters integrated out rather than point-estimated. Took BO from theory to a tool practitioners actually used.

A Tutorial on Bayesian Optimization

Frazier·2018best on-ramp

The best on-ramp: clean derivation of GP surrogates, Expected Improvement, knowledge gradient, and when BO is (and isn't) the right tool.

Taking the Human Out of the Loop

Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams, de Freitas·2016·Proc. IEEE

Broader survey than Frazier — alternative acquisition functions, non-GP surrogates, constrained/multi-fidelity/parallel BO, and applications.

Bayesian Optimization

Garnett·2023·textbook

The reference textbook: full mathematical treatment of GPs, acquisition theory, computation, multi-fidelity/multi-objective/constrained extensions. The spine for a BO workshop curriculum.

07

Causal Bayesian optimization

Intervene on any node of a known causal graph, not just the target variable directly — the classic-BO analog to "which marketing lever, given last quarter's causal effects, in a market that keeps shifting."

Causal Bayesian Optimization

Aglietti, Lu, Paleyes, González·2020·AISTATS

Introduces CBO: intervene on any node in a known causal graph via do-calculus, not just the target directly. Sample-efficiency comes from graph structure — upstream interventions can be informative even when a downstream target is what's optimized.

Dynamic Causal Bayesian Optimization

Aglietti, Dhir, González, Damoulas·2021·NeurIPS

Extends CBO to systems where both target and causal structure evolve over time — each step picks a locally optimal intervention from past observational and interventional data.

08

LLM-augmented BO

The surrogate's role goes from absent, to assisted, to disciplined, to fully agentic. Read in order to watch how much trust each design puts in the LLM.

Reading order: 1 OPRO (no surrogate) → 2 LLAMBO (LLM assists a real surrogate) → 3 LABO (two-phase discipline) → 4 "Are We There Yet?" (skeptical audit) → 5 Agentic BO / Sara-lenz (fullest synthesis).
1

OPRO: Large Language Models as Optimizers

Yang, Wang, Lu, Liu, Le, Zhou, Chen — Google DeepMind·2023baseline

The foundational "LLM-as-optimizer" paper: put the trajectory of past (solution, score) pairs in a prompt, have the LLM propose the next one directly — no explicit surrogate at all. The minimal baseline every other paper here extends or argues against.

2

LLAMBO

Liu, Astorga, Seedat, van der Schaar·2024·ICLR

A disciplined middle ground: keeps the real surrogate + acquisition structure, lets the LLM contribute at specific points — zero-shot warmstarting, candidate proposals, domain-context enrichment. This is the baseline the Agentic BO paper benchmarks against.

3

LABO

Chen, Yuan, Zhang, et al.·2026

Splits the LLM's role into two explicit phases — broad exploration, then selective, statistically-grounded experimentation — to avoid the overconfident, memorization-prone proposals of letting an LLM freely drive the whole loop.

4

LLMs for BO in Scientific Domains: Are We There Yet?

Gupta, Hartford, Liu·2025skeptical

A critical evaluation of LLM-driven BO in scientific settings — tests whether LLM surrogates/proposers actually help over classic GP-based BO, and where they fall short: calibration, exploration discipline, benchmark memorization.

5

Agentic BO: Sara / lenz

Brunzema, Tiao, Le, De Angeli, Xuan, Gligorijevic — Meta·2026

An LLM agent ("Sara") drives a BO campaign — reading natural-language priors, inspecting surrogate diagnostics, reconfiguring search space or objective mid-run — while a BoTorch backend ("lenz") does the actual math via CLI. Because lenz keeps a raw trial log as ground truth, the agent can change the problem definition mid-campaign without invalidating prior data. Also documents "benchmark recognition" — LLMs identifying Ackley/Hartmann test functions from training-data memorization.

Part III

Who Decides — Agentic & Autonomous Research

Not a statistical method — a question of control. What happens when an LLM, or a team of them, owns hypothesis generation and experiment design end-to-end, with no fixed algorithmic policy underneath? Two modules: free-form multi-agent "co-scientist" teams, and LLM-as-mutation-operator evolutionary program search.

09

AI co-scientists & autonomous research agents

None of these are chemistry/materials-specific — general-purpose or ML-research loops, on brief. Never present the co-scientist papers without the critique paper alongside them — it plays the same role here that the Trust Crisis paper plays for SBI calibration.

Read the survey first and the critique last — everything in between is easier to evaluate once you've seen both the frame and the rebuttal.
1

From AI for Science to Agentic Science

Wei, Yang, Zhang, Chen, Zhuang, et al.·2025read first

The orientation piece for this module — unifies process-, autonomy-, and mechanism-oriented views of "agentic science," surveying realizations across life sciences, chemistry, materials, physics.

AI Co-Scientist

Gottweis, Weng, Daryin, Tu, et al. — Google DeepMind·2025·Nature 2026

Multi-agent system on Gemini that generates, critiques, ranks, and evolves hypotheses through a tournament-style "generate, debate, evolve" loop, grounded in literature retrieval and external tools. The most institutionally-backed exemplar of the pattern.

Robin

Ghareeb, Chang, Mitchener, Yiu, et al. — FutureHouse·2025·Nature 2026

Biology-domain agent team — literature agents "Crow"/"Falcon" plus data-analysis agent "Finch" — running an iterative lab-in-the-loop cycle to a real repurposed drug candidate for a retinal disease. Agentic discovery, outside chemistry/materials.

The AI Scientist

Sakana AI·2024

End-to-end automation of ML research itself — idea generation, coding, experiments, plotting, full paper write-up, simulated peer review. Closes the loop on the entire research lifecycle, not one step of it.

The AI Scientist-v2

Sakana AI·2025

Drops v1's human-authored code templates, generalizes across ML subfields via progressive agentic tree search, produced the first fully AI-generated paper to pass a real workshop peer-review bar.

Agent Laboratory

Schmidgall, et al.·2025

Role-based agent team (PhD / Postdoc / ML-Engineer / Professor) running literature review → experimentation → report writing, with an explicit human-checkpoint "co-pilot" mode. Reports an 84% cost reduction versus prior autonomous-research baselines.

AgentRxiv

Schmidgall, et al.·2025

Extends Agent Laboratory into a shared preprint server for agents — one lab's findings become immediately available as context to other autonomous labs. Tests whether agent research systems compound the way human science does via citation.

8

Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Discovery

Bisht, Kumar, Jablonka, Mausam, Krishnan·2026read last

The load-bearing counterweight. Problem selection isn't neutral (the McNamara fallacy — optimizing what's measurable over what matters); LLM training corpora omit tacit lab/procedural failure-knowledge; preference optimization compresses idea diversity; most benchmarks never feed real physical-experiment results back into the loop.

10

Evolutionary program search agents

Structurally distinct from both BO and free-form co-scientist teams: an LLM as the mutation/crossover operator over a population of candidate programs, scored by a hard evaluator. No posterior, no calibrated belief about where the optimum is — just selection pressure. Good material to contrast directly against Part II: same propose → evaluate → update skeleton, very different epistemics.

FunSearch

Romera-Paredes, Barekatain, Novikov, et al. — Google DeepMind·2023·Nature

Pairs a pretrained LLM with a systematic evaluator in an evolutionary loop: the LLM sees its best programs so far and proposes an improved one, the evaluator scores it, survivors carry into the next generation's context. Found genuinely new constructions for the cap set problem and better bin-packing heuristics — real open-problem progress, not benchmark-matching.

AlphaEvolve

Novikov, Vũ, Eisenberger, et al. — Google DeepMind·2025

FunSearch's generalization from single functions to entire codebases — improved matrix-multiplication algorithms, data-center job scheduling, alongside open math problems. The clearest evidence the pattern scales past toy problems into production systems.

Barbarians at the Gate (ADRS)

Cheng, Liu, Pan, Li, Wang, et al. — UC Berkeley·2025

Introduces ADRS — the same generate/evaluate/refine loop applied to computer-systems algorithm design: cloud scheduling, MoE load balancing, transaction scheduling. Across ten case studies, ADRS-generated solutions matched or beat human state-of-the-art, up to 5.0× faster or 26% cheaper. The systems-research analog of FunSearch/AlphaEvolve's math focus.

AlphaLab

Hogan, Chen, Wilson, Rasul, et al. — Morgan Stanley·2026

A general-purpose harness: given a dataset and a natural-language objective, runs data exploration, self-built adversarially-validated evaluation, then GPU experiments via a Strategist/Worker split. Across CUDA kernels, LLM pretraining, and traffic forecasting, GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6 found qualitatively different solutions in every domain — multi-model campaigns cover more search space than one model alone.

Companion repo — no paper attached
repos/autoresearch (Karpathy, March 2026) — the reference for "auto research" as popularized outside the papers above. Three files: train.py (single-GPU nanochat-style training loop, the only file the agent edits), prepare.py (fixed data/tokenizer prep), program.md (plain-English instructions, the only file the human edits). Loop: propose an edit → train 5 minutes → measure val_bpb → keep or roll back → repeat. Karpathy's own overnight run: ~700 experiments, 20 stacked improvements. By far the smallest, most workshop-ready implementation of this pattern — runs on one GPU, a real live-coding candidate rather than a reading.
Excluded, on purpose

"AutoResearch-RL" (arXiv 2603.07300) — not included

Evaluated and rejected: its author block credits individuals with three simultaneous institutional affiliations each, and lists "Claude AI" as a co-author attributed to the wrong company (DeepMind, not Anthropic). Synthesis

Open frontiers

Two gaps turned up across every search pass in this library. Both are genuinely unclaimed — the tooling and the closest analogs exist on both sides of each gap; nobody has connected them yet.

Gap 1 — of 2

Amortized inference for Media Mix Modeling

Nothing published connects SBI/ABI tooling to marketing econometrics. metabeta is the closest structural analog (hierarchical regression, amortized); Jin et al. and NNN are the marketing-side status quo. The domain, the math, and the software all exist separately. See Module 05.

Gap 2 — of 2

Agentic control of an amortized-inference pipeline

Every agentic-optimization paper in Module 08–10 targets either a GP-based BO backend or a fully model-free research loop. None wire an LLM agent up to a BayesFlow/sbi-style amortized posterior as the object of agentic control. Currently zero prior art to cite against — the sharpest identified white space across the whole library, and the natural synthesis chapter tying all three parts together.
