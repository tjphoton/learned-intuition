# Learned Intuition — Paper Library — Summary & Map

*Simulation-Based Inference, Bayesian Optimization, and the Rise of Agentic Research*

Organized library for a planned book + 2-day hands-on workshop on **Simulation-Based Inference (SBI) / Amortized Bayesian Inference (ABI)**, **Bayesian Optimization (BO)**, and **Agentic / Autonomous Research**. This file indexes every PDF currently in the repo, grouped by folder, with a one-line-to-short-paragraph summary and why it matters for the book/workshop. ## 1. `Bayesian-Optimization/` — Foundations

Classic, pre-LLM Bayesian optimization. This is the "calculator" — the math for deciding what to try next under an expensive black-box objective.

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Efficient Global Optimization of Expensive Black-Box Functions** (Jones, Schonlau, Welch, 1998) | The founding EGO paper. Introduces the Expected Improvement (EI) acquisition function and Kriging (Gaussian Process) surrogates for optimizing expensive black-box functions. Everything downstream in BO traces back to this. |
| **Practical Bayesian Optimization of Machine Learning Algorithms** (Snoek, Larochelle, Adams, 2012 — "Spearmint") | Made BO practical for ML hyperparameter tuning: handles noisy observations, parallel/async evaluation, and integrates out GP hyperparameters instead of point-estimating them. The paper that took BO from theory to a tool ML practitioners actually used. |
| **A Tutorial on Bayesian Optimization** (Frazier, 2018) | The best on-ramp. Clean derivation of GP surrogates, EI, knowledge gradient, and when BO is/isn't the right tool (continuous domains, <20 dims, expensive noisy evaluations). Good first read before the book. |
| **Taking the Human Out of the Loop: A Review of Bayesian Optimization** (Shahriari, Swersky, Wang, Adams, de Freitas, *Proc. IEEE* 2016) | Broader survey — covers alternative acquisition functions, non-GP surrogates, constrained/multi-fidelity/parallel BO, and applications. More comprehensive than Frazier, less tutorial-paced. |
| **Bayesian Optimization** (Garnett, textbook, 2023) | The reference textbook. Full mathematical treatment: GPs, acquisition function theory, computation, extensions (multi-fidelity, multi-objective, constraints). This is the "read cover-to-cover if serious" book — treat as the spine for the BO half of the workshop curriculum. |

**Gap check:** solid on fundamentals. Missing: a hands-on-coding-friendly reference (e.g., BoTorch/Ax tutorials aren't here as PDFs — pull from docs, not papers, when building exercises).

### `Bayesian-Optimization/Causal-Bayesian-Optimization/`

Background thread bridging BO and causal inference — where an agent intervenes on nodes of a known (or partially known) causal graph rather than treating the objective as a plain black box.

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Causal Bayesian Optimization** (Aglietti, Lu, Paleyes, González, *AISTATS* 2020) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Introduces CBO: instead of only querying the target variable directly, the algorithm can intervene on *any* node in a known causal graph, using do-calculus to propagate the effect through the system. Sample-efficiency win comes from exploiting graph structure — interventions on upstream nodes can be informative even when the downstream target is what's actually optimized. |
| **Dynamic Causal Bayesian Optimization** (Aglietti, Dhir, González, Damoulas, *NeurIPS* 2021) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Extends CBO to systems where both the target and the causal structure evolve over time — at each time step it picks a locally optimal intervention using both past observational and past interventional data. Structurally the closest classic-BO analog to "which marketing lever to pull this quarter, given last quarter's causal effects and a market that keeps shifting." |

## 2. `Simulation-Based-Inference/` — organized into 6 sub-topics

This is "Idea 1" from `research.md`: instead of computing a posterior from scratch every time (MCMC), train a network on millions of simulated (parameters → data) pairs so it learns to recognize the answer, then run inference in a single forward pass ("amortized"). Three historically separate lineages converge here: **SBI** (physics/astro), **ABI** (stats/cog-sci, BayesFlow), and **PFNs** (AutoML, TabPFN). `research.md` §"three names, one idea" is the canonical explainer — worth adapting almost verbatim into the book's intro chapter.

### 2.1 `01-Foundations-and-Reviews/`

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **The Frontier of Simulation-Based Inference** (Cranmer, Brehmer, Louppe, *PNAS* 2020) | The field-defining survey from the physics/astro lineage. Frames the likelihood-free inference problem, traces ABC → neural density estimation, and previews the "learn the posterior/likelihood/likelihood-ratio directly" taxonomy that later papers all specialize. **Read this first** — it's the shared vocabulary for everything else in the folder. |
| **Diffusion Models in Simulation-Based Inference: A Tutorial Review** (Arruda, Bracher, Köthe, Hasenauer, 2025) | Modern generative-modeling layer under SBI/ABI: diffusion, flow matching, consistency models as alternatives to normalizing flows. Explains score-based "superpowers" (prior adaptation without retraining, compositional aggregation across many observations). Best single orientation piece on *how* the neural network inside BayesFlow/metabeta actually represents the posterior. |
| **Neural Methods for Amortized Inference** (Zammit-Mangion, Sainsbury-Dale, Huser, *Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application* 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* The closest thing to a field-wide unifying survey — explicitly bridges the SBI / ABI / PFN naming split (point estimation, approximate Bayesian inference, summary-statistic construction, likelihood approximation, all under one "amortized inference" umbrella) with a worked illustrative example. Strong candidate for the book's Chapter 1 anchor citation, precisely because it doesn't pick a lineage. |

### 2.2 `02-Core-ABI-Methods-and-Software/`

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **metabeta: A fast neural model for Bayesian Mixed-Effects Regression** (Kipnis, Binz, Schulz, 2025) | The closest existing analog to an amortized Marketing Mix Model — hierarchical (fixed + random effects) regression solved via neural posterior estimation, matching HMC accuracy at ~0.01s vs ~37s. Uses local + global summary networks, normalizing-flow posteriors, plus post-hoc importance sampling and conformal prediction for calibration. **Read this closely; it's the dress rehearsal.** |
| **BayesFlow: Amortized Bayesian Workflows with Neural Networks** (Radev, Schmitt, Schumacher, et al., 2023) | Original BayesFlow paper/library. Establishes the prior → simulator → summary network → inference network pipeline and "amortized Bayesian workflow" framing. |
| **BayesFlow 2: Multi-Backend Amortized Bayesian Inference in Python** (Kühmichel et al., 2026) | Major rewrite on Keras 3 (JAX/PyTorch/TensorFlow backends). Adds diffusion/flow-matching/consistency-model inference networks, native hierarchical (partial-pooling) model support, and a first-class Diagnostics module (SBC, misspecification/simulation-gap detection). This is the **workhorse library** — treat as the primary coding-exercise tool for the ABI half of the workshop. |
| **Amortized Bayesian Mixture Models** (Kucharský, Bürkner, 2025) | Worked example of the ABI workflow applied to finite mixture models (label-switching, high-dim posteriors). Good as a second, simpler worked exercise before tackling metabeta-level hierarchy. |
| **Simformer: All-in-one Simulation-Based Inference** (Gloeckler, Deistler, Weilbach, Wood, Macke, 2024) | Transformer-based SBI that handles missing data and flexible conditioning (condition on any subset of parameters/data at inference time) via masking. Relevant because real commercial data (e.g., brand panels) is rarely complete — this is the exercise for "what if some weeks/features are missing." |
| **sbi: A toolkit for simulation-based inference** (Tejero-Cantero, Boelts, Deistler, Lueckmann, Durkan, Gonçalves, Greenberg, Macke, *JOSS* 2020) | ★ *Newly acquired.* The original paper for the other major SBI library (Macke lab, PyTorch-based). Lower-level than BayesFlow — exposes the density-estimator choice directly (NPE/NLE/NRE algorithm families) rather than a single high-level workflow API. Good second-library exercise once BayesFlow's higher-level abstractions are familiar. |
| **sbi reloaded: a toolkit for simulation-based inference workflows** (Boelts, Deistler, Gloeckler, Tejero-Cantero, et al., 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Major rewrite of `sbi` — expands from an algorithm library into a full workflow toolkit (mirrors BayesFlow 2's own evolution). Track this alongside BayesFlow 2 as the two competing "production ABI framework" reference implementations. |

### 2.3 `03-Calibration-and-Trust/` — **don't skip this tier**

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Validating Bayesian Inference Algorithms with Simulation-Based Calibration** (Talts, Betancourt, Simpson, Vehtari, Gelman, 2018) | The diagnostic standard (SBC). Procedure: simulate params → simulate data → run inference → check rank statistics of true param within the recovered posterior are uniform. Any amortized model deployed for real decisions needs this. Core hands-on exercise: implement SBC rank histograms from scratch. |
| **A Trust Crisis in Simulation-Based Inference? Your Posterior Approximations Can Be Unfaithful** (Hermans, Delaunoy, et al., *TMLR* 2022) | The load-bearing warning for the whole book. Nearly all SBI algorithms tested (NPE, SNL, ...) produced **overconfident** posteriors (credible intervals too narrow) in at least one benchmark setting — worst in the low-simulation-budget regime, which is exactly when amortized methods are most tempting to use. Remedy proposed: deep ensembling (5+ independently trained networks) to separate epistemic from aleatoric uncertainty. |
| **Calibrating Neural SBI with Differentiable Coverage Probability** (Falkiewicz, Takeishi, Shekhzadeh, Wehenkel, Delaunoy, Louppe, Kalousis, 2023) | Makes coverage itself a differentiable training objective rather than a post-hoc diagnostic — bakes calibration into training instead of checking it after the fact. |
| **Robust Amortized Bayesian Inference with Self-Consistency Losses on Unlabeled Data** (Mishra, Habermann, Schmitt, Radev, Bürkner, *ICLR* 2026) | Addresses the "simulation gap": what happens when real data doesn't look like the training simulations. Semi-supervised approach — let the model also learn from real, unlabeled data via self-consistency, improving robustness where it's evaluated far from its simulated training distribution. |

### 2.4 `04-Causal-SBI/` — promising, not yet proven on real-world data

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Do-PFN: In-Context Learning for Causal Effect Estimation** (Robertson, Reuter, Guo, Hollmann, Hutter, Schölkopf, *NeurIPS* 2025) | Trains a transformer on millions of synthetic Structural Causal Models (paired observational + interventional data) so it can predict **conditional interventional distributions** p(y | do(t), x) zero-shot, without knowing the causal graph at inference time. Correctly performs back-door/front-door-style adjustment implicitly. Evidence is synthetic/semi-synthetic only — no real-world validation yet. |
| **CausalPFN: Amortized Causal Effect Estimation via In-Context Learning** (Balazadeh, Kamkari, Thomas, et al., 2025) | Same PFN-for-causality idea as Do-PFN but targets the **expectation** (CEPO) rather than the full interventional distribution, and explicitly enforces identifiability in the training prior. Tested on real causal benchmarks (IHDP, LaLonde, ACIC) plus real uplift-modeling tasks — more real-data validation than Do-PFN. |
| **Interventional Time Series Priors for Causal Foundation Models** (Thumm, Chen, *ICLR 2026 Workshop*) | Extends the causal-PFN idea to **time series** — synthetic priors with regime-switching and time-varying interventions ("CausalTimePrior"). Structurally the closest thing in the library to "a brand's sales trajectory under a marketing intervention." High-relevance if the eventual application involves dynamic market systems. |

### 2.5 `05-Tabular-Foundation-Models/` — why the underlying recipe works at all

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference** (Müller, Hollmann, Pineda, Grabocka, Hutter, *ICLR* 2022) | ★ *Newly acquired.* The origin paper for **Prior-Data Fitted Networks (PFNs)** — introduces the core recipe (train a transformer via in-context learning to approximate Bayesian inference over a prior, in a single forward pass) that TabPFN, Do-PFN, and CausalPFN all inherit. Demonstrates PFNs near-perfectly mimicking Gaussian process posteriors and giving 200×+ speedups over classic approximate-inference methods. **Read this before TabPFN** — it's the mechanism, TabPFN is the scaled-up application. |
| **TabPFN: Accurate predictions on small data with a tabular foundation model** (Hollmann, Müller, Purucker, et al., *Nature* 2025) | The paradigm-proof. A transformer trained on ~130M synthetic tasks (generated via SCMs) does in-context learning over tabular data — a single forward pass beats an ensemble of XGBoost/CatBoost/LightGBM tuned for 4 hours. Cell-level tokenization, two-way (row/column) attention. Establishes that "train on synthetic tasks, recognize the answer at inference time" scales and wins on real benchmarks — the empirical backbone of the whole book's thesis. |
| **TabPFN-2.5: Advancing the State of the Art in Tabular Foundation Models** (Prior Labs, 2025) | Scale-up: 50,000 rows / 2,000 features (20× TabPFN v2). |
| **TabPFN-3: Technical Report** (Prior Labs, 2026) | Further scale-up to 1M training rows, adds a "Thinking" (test-time compute) mode, extends to time-series/relational/tabular-text data. |
| **From Tables to Time: Extending TabPFN-v2 to Time Series Forecasting** (Hoo, Müller, 2026) | Ports the tabular ICL recipe to covariate-informed forecasting (external regressors like weather/economic indicators). Relevant to any marketing time-series application. |
| **TabICLv2: A better, faster, scalable, and open tabular foundation model** (Qu, Holzmüller, Varoquaux, Le Morvan, 2026) | Current open SOTA on TabArena, beating RealTabPFN-2.5 and tuned CatBoost/XGBoost without dataset-specific tuning. Key tricks: Query-Aware Scalable Softmax (fixes attention fading at scale), repeated feature grouping (avoids representation collapse), a richer synthetic prior (random Cauchy graphs, GPs), Muon optimizer, disk-offloading for million-row tables on modest GPU memory, quantile regression (999 quantiles) for full predictive distributions. Fully open weights + inference code — good for reproducible workshop exercises. |
| **Effortless, Simulation-Efficient Bayesian Inference using Tabular Foundation Models** (Vetter, Gloeckler, Gedon, Macke, *NeurIPS* 2025) | The shortcut worth knowing: uses **TabPFN itself** as a generic SBI engine (feed simulated (θ, x) pairs as a "table," let TabPFN's in-context learning do the posterior approximation) instead of training a bespoke BayesFlow-style network. Much lower engineering lift — good candidate for the workshop's "fastest path to a working amortized posterior" exercise. |

### 2.6 `06-Marketing-and-Econometrics-Applications/` — the gap

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **Bayesian Methods for Media Mix Modeling with Carryover and Shape Effects** (Jin, Wang, Sun, Chan, Koehler — Google, 2017) | The incumbent industry standard for MMM. Hierarchical Bayesian regression with adstock (carryover/lag) and Hill-function-style saturation (diminishing returns), fit via MCMC, one brand/model at a time. This is the thing amortized inference would replace — read it as the baseline to benchmark against, not as a method to imitate mechanically. |
| **NNN: Next-Generation Neural Networks for Marketing Measurement** (Mulc, Anderson, Cubre, Zhang, Liu, Kumar — Google, 2025) | The one existing neural alternative to classic MMM, and it goes the *opposite* direction from amortized inference: a big Transformer trained directly on a brand's own data that **abandons explicit priors/parametric adstock-saturation** entirely, trading interpretability and uncertainty quantification for flexibility. Useful as the foil/counterpoint when explaining why amortized SBI (which keeps priors, just moves them into a simulator) is a different bet. |
| **Amortized Inference for Correlated Discrete Choice Models via Equivariant Neural Networks** (Huch, Keane, 2026) | The only amortized-inference paper found touching marketing/econometrics directly (discrete choice models, not MMM). Confirms the porting-the-PFN-recipe trend is real but hasn't reached MMM yet — this is the closest thing to prior art for "amortized inference for a commercial marketing model." |

**Gap check (confirmed empty, per `research.md` investigation):** there is no published amortized-inference / SBI paper for Media Mix Modeling specifically. The tooling (BayesFlow, `sbi`), the closest structural analog (metabeta), and the domain (marketing econometrics) all exist separately; nobody has connected them. This is the identified white space for original work/book content.

## 3. `Agentic-Research-and-Autonomous-Optimization/` — "Idea 2": which experiment to run next

This is the other half of the original framing: not *how to compute an answer fast*, but *how to decide what to try next*, with an LLM steering the process rather than a fixed algorithmic policy. Following the dedicated literature pass, this folder now splits into three natural sub-threads: **LLMs wired into a classic BO loop** (the LLM proposes/reasons, a statistical backend still does the math), **fully agentic "AI scientist" loops** (the LLM/agent owns hypothesis generation, experiment design, and interpretation end-to-end, usually with no GP surrogate in sight), and **LLM-driven evolutionary program search** (the LLM is the mutation operator over a population of candidate programs, scored by a hard evaluator — no surrogate, no posterior, just selection pressure).

### 3.1 `01-LLM-Augmented-Bayesian-Optimization/`

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **OPRO: Large Language Models as Optimizers** (Yang, Wang, Lu, Liu, Le, Zhou, Chen — Google DeepMind, 2023) | ★ *Newly acquired.* The foundational "LLM-as-optimizer" paper: describes the optimization problem and trajectory of past (solution, score) pairs in a prompt, and has the LLM propose the next solution directly from that context — no explicit surrogate model at all. Demonstrated on linear regression, TSP, and prompt optimization (beats human-designed prompts by up to 50% on Big-Bench Hard). The minimal baseline every other paper in this folder either extends or argues against. |
| **LLAMBO: Large Language Models to Enhance Bayesian Optimization** (Liu, Astorga, Seedat, van der Schaar, *ICLR* 2024) | ★ *Newly acquired.* A more disciplined middle ground than OPRO: keeps the BO loop's structure (surrogate + acquisition) but lets the LLM contribute at specific pressure points — zero-shot warmstarting, proposing candidate points in natural language, and enriching the surrogate with domain context — especially valuable when observations are sparse. This is the LLAMBO cited as a baseline inside the Sara/lenz paper below; read it first to understand what Sara is being benchmarked against. |
| **LABO: LLM-Accelerated Bayesian Optimization through Broad Exploration and Selective Experimentation** (Chen, Yuan, Zhang, et al., 2026) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Splits the LLM's role into two explicit phases — broad, LLM-driven exploration of the space, then selective, statistically-grounded experimentation — arguing this two-phase separation avoids the overconfident, memorization-prone proposals that come from letting an LLM freely drive the whole loop (per OPRO-style methods). Recent enough to reflect current best practice. |
| **LLMs for Bayesian Optimization in Scientific Domains: Are We There Yet?** (Gupta, Hartford, Liu, 2025) | A critical evaluation of LLM-driven BO in scientific settings — tests whether LLMs used as surrogates/proposers for BO actually help versus classic GP-based BO, and where they fall short (calibration, exploration discipline, benchmark memorization). Good skeptical counterweight to read before the Sara/lenz paper below. |
| **Agentic Bayesian Optimization through Surrogate-Augmented Autoresearch** (Brunzema, Tiao, Le, De Angeli, Xuan, Gligorijevic — Meta, 2026) | Introduces **Agentic BO**: an LLM agent ("Sara") that drives a BO campaign — reading natural-language priors, inspecting surrogate diagnostics (e.g., cross-validated R²), reconfiguring search space/acquisition function/objective mid-run — while a structured BoTorch-based backend ("lenz") does the actual GP/acquisition-function math via a CLI. Because lenz keeps a raw trial log as ground truth, the agent can **change the problem definition mid-campaign without invalidating prior data** (e.g., promoting a constraint to a second objective). Benchmarked against standard BO (Ax) and LLM-only optimizers (LLAMBO): matches classic BO with no informative prior, and beats it when a natural-language prior is available. Also documents "benchmark recognition" (LLMs identifying Ackley/Hartmann-style test functions from training-data memorization) — a methodological trap to teach explicitly in the workshop. |

**Reading order within this subfolder:** OPRO (baseline, no surrogate) → LLAMBO (LLM assists a real surrogate) → LABO (explicit two-phase discipline) → "Are We There Yet?" (skeptical audit) → Sara/lenz (fullest synthesis, plus mid-campaign reformulation). This progression is itself a good spine for a workshop module on "how much should you trust the LLM inside the optimization loop."

### 3.2 `02-Autonomous-Research-Agents-and-AI-Co-Scientists/`

Fully agentic systems where the LLM (or a multi-agent team of them) owns the scientific loop end-to-end — hypothesis generation, experiment/analysis design, execution, and write-up — rather than just steering a BO backend. None of these are chemistry/materials-science-specific; they're general-purpose or ML-research-specific, per the brief.

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **From AI for Science to Agentic Science: A Survey on Autonomous Scientific Discovery** (Wei, Yang, Zhang, Chen, Zhuang, et al., 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* The orientation piece for this subfolder — unifies process-oriented, autonomy-oriented, and mechanism-oriented views of "agentic science" into one framework, and surveys realizations across life sciences, chemistry, materials, and physics. **Read this first**, same role Cranmer plays for the SBI folder. |
| **AI Co-Scientist** (Gottweis, Weng, Daryin, Tu, et al. — Google DeepMind/Google Research, 2025; published in *Nature* 2026) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Multi-agent system built on Gemini that generates, critiques, ranks, and evolves research hypotheses through a tournament-style "generate, debate, evolve" loop, grounded in literature retrieval and external tools rather than relying on parametric memory alone. The most institutionally-backed exemplar of the "AI co-scientist" pattern named in the brief. |
| **Robin: A multi-agent system for automating scientific discovery** (Ghareeb, Chang, Mitchener, Yiu, et al. — FutureHouse, 2025; *Nature* 2026) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Biology-domain agent team (literature-review agents "Crow"/"Falcon" + data-analysis agent "Finch") running an iterative lab-in-the-loop cycle of hypothesis → experiment proposal → real wet-lab data interpretation → refined hypothesis. Used to identify a real repurposed drug candidate for a retinal disease. Directly on-brief: agentic discovery loop, outside chemistry/materials science. |
| **The AI Scientist: Towards Fully Automated Open-Ended Scientific Discovery** (Sakana AI, 2024) | ★ *Newly acquired.* End-to-end automation of *ML research itself*: idea generation, coding, running experiments, plotting, writing up a full paper, and a simulated peer-review pass — closing the loop on the entire research lifecycle, not just one step of it. |
| **The AI Scientist-v2: Workshop-Level Automated Scientific Discovery via Agentic Tree Search** (Sakana AI, 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Successor that drops the human-authored code templates v1 depended on, generalizes across ML subfields via progressive agentic tree search, and produced the first fully AI-generated paper to pass a real peer-review bar at a workshop. Good paired reading with v1 to see what "removing scaffolding" costs and buys. |
| **Agent Laboratory: Using LLM Agents as Research Assistants** (Schmidgall et al., 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Role-based agent team (PhD/Postdoc/ML-Engineer/Professor personas) that runs literature review → experimentation → report writing, with an explicit "co-pilot" mode for human checkpoints — a more human-in-the-loop-friendly design than the fully autonomous systems above. Reports an 84% cost reduction versus prior autonomous-research baselines. |
| **AgentRxiv: Towards Collaborative Autonomous Research** (Schmidgall et al., 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Extends Agent Laboratory into a shared preprint server *for agents* — one lab's autonomously-generated findings become immediately available as context to other autonomous labs, testing whether agent research systems can compound on each other's output the way human science does via citation. Relevant if the workshop wants to discuss multi-agent, multi-lab research economies rather than single-agent loops. |
| **Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Scientific Discovery** (Bisht, Kumar, Jablonka, Mausam, Krishnan, 2026) | ★ *Newly acquired.* **The load-bearing skeptical counterweight for this folder** — plays the same role here that Hermans et al. plays for SBI calibration. Argues current agentic-AI-scientist systems have structural blind spots: problem selection is not neutral (the "McNamara fallacy" — optimizing what's measurable over what matters), LLM training corpora omit tacit lab/procedural failure-knowledge, preference optimization compresses idea diversity, and most benchmarks never feed real physical-experiment results back into the loop. **Do not present the AI-co-scientist papers above without this one alongside them.** |

### 3.3 `03-Evolutionary-Program-Search-Agents/`

A structurally distinct third sub-thread, worth keeping visually separate from `01/`: instead of a GP surrogate + acquisition function (BO) or a free-form multi-agent research team (co-scientist systems), these pair an LLM with an **evolutionary loop over a population of candidate programs**, scored by a hard automated evaluator. No uncertainty quantification, no posterior — the LLM is the mutation/crossover operator, and selection pressure does the rest. Good material for the workshop to contrast explicitly against BO: same "propose → evaluate → update" skeleton, very different epistemics (no calibrated belief about *where* the optimum is, just a scored population).

See also `repos/autoresearch/` (Code repositories, below) — Karpathy's minimal single-GPU implementation of exactly this pattern, and the most workshop-ready hands-on exercise of the three (no accompanying paper, repo only).

**Deliberately excluded:** a paper titled "AutoResearch-RL: Perpetual Self-Evaluating Reinforcement Learning Agents for Autonomous Neural Architecture Discovery" (arXiv 2603.07300, posted the same day `autoresearch` went viral) was evaluated and rejected — its author block credits individuals with three simultaneous institutional affiliations each and lists "Claude AI" as a co-author attributed to the wrong company (DeepMind, not Anthropic). Treat as likely fabricated/satirical trend-riding content, not a citable source. Noted here so it isn't re-considered later without this context.

| File | Summary |
|---|---|
| **FunSearch: Mathematical discoveries from program search with large language models** (Romera-Paredes, Barekatain, Novikov, et al. — Google DeepMind, *Nature* 2023) | ★ *Newly acquired.* Pairs a pretrained LLM with a systematic evaluator in an evolutionary procedure: the LLM sees its best programs so far and proposes an improved one, the evaluator scores it, and the best survive into the next generation's context. Found genuinely new, previously-unknown constructions for the cap set problem (extremal combinatorics) and better bin-packing heuristics — real open-problem progress, not benchmark-matching. This is almost certainly the paper you were thinking of. |
| **AlphaEvolve: A coding agent for scientific and algorithmic discovery** (Novikov, Vũ, Eisenberger, et al. — Google DeepMind, 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired.* FunSearch's successor and generalization — evolves entire codebases/algorithms rather than single mathematical functions, and targets real infrastructure (improved matrix-multiplication algorithms, data-center job scheduling) alongside open math problems. The clearest evidence yet that "LLM + evolutionary search + hard evaluator" scales past toy problems into production systems. Read directly after FunSearch to see the one-function → whole-codebase generalization. |
| **Barbarians at the Gate: How AI is Upending Systems Research** (Cheng, Liu, Pan, Li, Wang, et al. — UC Berkeley, incl. Zaharia, Stoica, 2025) | ★ *Newly acquired, at your request.* Introduces **ADRS** (AI-Driven Research for Systems): the same generate → evaluate → refine loop as FunSearch/AlphaEvolve, applied specifically to *computer-systems* algorithm design (multi-region cloud scheduling, mixture-of-experts load balancing, LLM-serving SQL, transaction scheduling). Across ten case studies, ADRS-generated solutions matched or beat human state-of-the-art designs — up to 5.0× faster or 26% cheaper — often within hours at low compute cost. The systems-research analog of FunSearch/AlphaEvolve's math/algorithms focus; useful for showing the pattern generalizes across domains, and a Berkeley-authored counterpart to the two DeepMind papers above. |
| **AlphaLab: Autonomous Multi-Agent Research Across Optimization Domains with Frontier LLMs** (Hogan, Chen, Wilson, Rasul, Boyarsky, Kamei, Schneider, Nevmyvaka — Morgan Stanley, 2026) | ★ *Newly acquired.* A more elaborate, general-purpose harness than Karpathy's `autoresearch`: given just a dataset and a natural-language objective, it runs a three-phase autonomous loop — data exploration/report writing, self-constructed and adversarially-validated evaluation frameworks, then large-scale GPU experiments via a Strategist/Worker agent split, accumulating a persistent "playbook" of domain knowledge across runs. Evaluated with GPT-5.2 and Claude Opus 4.6 across three genuinely different domains — CUDA kernel optimization (4.4× faster than `torch.compile` on average, up to 91× on some kernels), LLM pretraining (22% lower validation loss than single-shot baseline), and traffic forecasting (23–25% over standard baselines). Notably, the two backing LLMs found qualitatively different solutions in every domain, suggesting multi-model campaigns cover more search space than any one model alone. A preprint (marked "under review" as of acquisition) but from a named, single-institution industry team — treat as credible, not yet peer-reviewed. ## Suggested reading order (updated)

1. Cranmer et al., *Frontier of SBI*, and/or Zammit-Mangion et al., *Neural Methods for Amortized Inference* (shared vocabulary across the SBI/ABI/PFN naming split)
2. Müller et al., *Transformers Can Do Bayesian Inference* (the PFN mechanism, before seeing it scaled up)
3. metabeta (closest structural analog to a hierarchical marketing/econometrics model)
4. BayesFlow 2, then `sbi`/`sbi reloaded` for comparison (the tools you'd actually build with)
5. Talts et al. SBC + Hermans et al. *Trust Crisis* (the calibration discipline — non-negotiable before trusting any amortized posterior)
6. Do-PFN / CausalPFN, then Causal Bayesian Optimization / Dynamic CBO (causal frontier, inference side then decision side)
7. TabPFN (Nature) → TabICLv2 (why the recipe works, current SOTA)
8. Jin et al. MMM + NNN (the marketing-side status quo and its neural foil)
9. OPRO → LLAMBO → LABO → Agentic BO (Sara/lenz) (the "what to try next" layer, once the "compute the answer fast" layer is solid — read in this order to see the surrogate's role go from absent, to assisted, to disciplined, to fully agentic)
10. "From AI for Science to Agentic Science" survey → AI Co-Scientist / Robin (the fully agentic end of the spectrum) → **"Agentic AI Scientists Are Not Built For Autonomous Scientific Discovery"** (read this last in the sequence, not skipped — it's the reality check on everything in step 10, the same role Hermans plays for SBI in step 5)
